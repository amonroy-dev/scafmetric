# Scaffold factors: research and limitations

Reviewed November 16, 2026. The current official OSHA pages for §§1926.450 and 1926.451 were accessible and used for the provisions identified below. The earlier review relied on a [third-party mirror of GPO eCFR title 29](https://github.com/xerj-org/corpus-ecfr-title-29/blob/main/parts/part-1926/sec-1926.451.txt). This review does not independently verify every scaffold requirement or the shoring provisions discussed below.

The 4:1 preset and user-entered custom safety factor are optional illustrative demand comparisons, not independently validated OSHA or ASD load combinations. For factor SF, the implemented uniform comparison loads are `D + SW + (SF / 1.67)L` for bending and `D + SW + (SF / 1.50)L` for shear, where D is the entered dead load and SW is beam self-weight. Entered live point loads receive the corresponding multiplier. For SF = 4, these multipliers are approximately 2.39521 and 2.66667; for SF = 2.5, they are approximately 1.49701 and 1.66667. Service response and deflection use `D + SW + L`, including unmultiplied point loads. Selected ASD bending and shear checks do not establish scaffold compliance.

[29 CFR 1926.451(a)(1)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451) generally requires a scaffold and its components to support their own weight plus at least four times the maximum intended load applied or transmitted, without failure, subject to the listed exceptions. [29 CFR 1926.450(b)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.450) includes persons, equipment, tools, materials, transmitted loads and other reasonably anticipated loads in maximum intended load. The calculator's dead/live input labels do not classify these loads for OSHA. Entered dead load can contain superimposed intended loads beyond the beam's own weight.

Different AISC limit states can use different ASD factors, and nominal strength is not automatically a demonstrated failure load. The implemented comparisons against `Rn / Ω` imply `Rn ≥ Ω(D + SW) + SF × L` for each supported limit state, using Ω = 1.67 for bending and Ω = 1.50 for the implemented shear case. This does not establish the required treatment of every applicable intended load. Applying the bending multiplier 4/1.67 to a shear allowable based on Ω = 1.50 would provide only about 3.593 times live load at the nominal-strength level; the implementation instead uses 4/1.50 for shear. Neither comparison multiplier is applied to service deflection.

[29 CFR 1926.451(f)(16)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451) limits loaded platform deflection to span/60. The [§1926.450(b) platform definition](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.450) concerns elevated work surfaces, including planks, decks and fabricated platforms; the distinction is the component's function, not whether it is made of steel. The cited platform provision does not establish this calculator's selected support-beam L/240 criterion. The calculator checks adjusted service-load deflection using 0.80 EI against L/240 by default, with L/360 and L/180 selectable. This is a preliminary serviceability criterion; a qualified designer must confirm the applicable project requirements and any additional deflection checks. A beam can meet the evaluated strength checks while failing the selected deflection limit. [§1926.451(a)(6)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451) requires qualified-person scaffold design and construction/loading consistent with that design.

The user supplied the original `ANSI+ASSP+A10.8-2019.pdf`, and its text was independently checked in this review. ANSI/ASSP A10.8-2019 §5.1.1.3, printed page 28 (PDF page 29 of 120), limits center deflection of a platform unit under design load to span/60. Section 5.1 on the same page applies to scaffold platforms and platform units, including planks, decks, fabricated platforms and modular platforms. This confirms the cited platform criterion; it does not establish the calculator's selected L/240 support-beam criterion. The supplied PDF remains outside the repository. The [official ANSI preview](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ASSE/preview_ANSI%2BASSP%2BA10.8-2019.pdf) identifies this edition and its contents but does not reproduce §5.1.1.3.

Relevant ANSI design context was also reviewed: §4.3, printed pages 21–22 (PDF pages 22–23), addresses component strength and maximum intended load; §4.8, printed page 23 (PDF page 24), addresses scaffold strength and its exceptions. Sections 12.1–12.2, printed pages 49–50 (PDF pages 50–51), address outrigger-scaffold engineering, intended cantilever loads and restraint. Section 15, printed page 52 (PDF page 53), addresses needle beams, and §16.3, printed page 53 (PDF page 54), requires engineering verification of supporting-structure safety factors for interior hung scaffolds. These reviewed provisions do not prescribe L/240 for this calculator's simply supported support-beam model. This limited review is not a determination that every supporting beam is exempt from other applicable deflection requirements. Confirm the scaffold arrangement, manufacturer instructions, project requirements and governing design criteria separately.

Other requirements depend on the scaffold component and situation. The earlier mirror-based review identified sixfold loading provisions for suspension ropes and connecting hardware in §1926.451(a)(3) and (4), tipping-moment requirements for direct connections and counterweights in §1926.451(a)(2), and tipping restraints associated with the 4:1 height-to-base ratio in §1926.451(c)(1). These are separate requirements; the height-to-base ratio is not the same as the strength factor. Confirm applicability before using any comparison as a design criterion.

The earlier mirror-based review of [29 CFR 1926.703(a)(1)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.703) found a requirement for covered formwork to support reasonably anticipated vertical and lateral loads without failure, rather than a universal 2.5 shoring factor. It also identified a 2.5-times-lifted-load requirement for jacking equipment during lift-slab operations in [29 CFR 1926.705(d)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.705). These two shoring-related provisions were not independently rechecked against the official source in this review. Other shoring standards, manufacturer requirements or local rules may apply. Confirm the relevant shoring type and governing source before adding a design check.
