# Scaffold factors: research and limitations

The user's proposed multipliers are 4 / 1.67 = 2.39520958… and 2.5 / 1.67 = 1.49700599…. These are arithmetic comparisons, not independently validated OSHA or ASD load combinations. The 4:1 preset and user-entered custom safety factor are available only as optional illustrative comparisons using the stated 1.67 divisor. The service-load response and deflection remain based on 1.0D + 1.0L; Selected ASD steel bending and shear checks are available, but they do not establish scaffold compliance. Point loads entered in the calculator are treated as live loads for these illustrative comparisons.

General scaffold provision: [29 CFR 1926.451(a)(1)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451) requires, with listed exceptions, capacity without failure for a scaffold or component's own weight plus at least four times the maximum intended load applied or transmitted to it. [29 CFR 1926.450(b)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.450) defines maximum intended load to include persons, equipment, tools, materials, transmitted loads, and other reasonably anticipated loads. The input fields do not determine which loads fall into that category. An entered dead load can contain superimposed loads beyond the beam's self-weight.

Direct OSHA/eCFR access was blocked in this environment. The quoted requirements were checked against a [third-party mirror of GPO eCFR title 29](https://github.com/xerj-org/corpus-ecfr-title-29/blob/main/parts/part-1926/sec-1926.451.txt), whose provenance identifies GPO bulk XML amended September 30, 2026. This is not verification against the current official source; confirm the provisions and applicable editions before implementing a code check.

A 1.67 ASD divisor is not universal: different AISC limit states can use different factors, and nominal strength is not automatically a demonstrated failure load. Comparing D + (4/1.67)L to Rn/1.67 requires Rn ≥ 1.67D + 4L, not automatically D_self + 4 × every applicable intended load. For example, where shear allowable strength uses Rn/1.5, 4/1.67 on live load translates to about 3.593 times that load at the nominal-strength level. Do not apply a factor intended for one limit state to every limit state or to service deflection.

Other requirements depend on the scaffold component and situation. The mirrored §1926.451(a)(3) and (4) specify six times relevant loading for suspension ropes and connecting hardware, with additional hoist-load conditions for adjustable scaffolds. Section 1926.451(a)(2) gives tipping-moment conditions for direct connections and counterweights. Section 1926.451(c)(1)'s 4:1 height-to-base ratio concerns tipping restraints, not the same strength factor. Section 1926.451(f)(16) limits scaffold platform deflection to 1/60 of span when loaded, which is not a general beam limit.

The mirrored [29 CFR 1926.703(a)(1)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.703) requires covered formwork to support reasonably anticipated vertical and lateral loads without failure; that paragraph does not state a universal 2.5 factor for shoring. The mirrored [29 CFR 1926.705(d)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.705) specifies 2.5 times the load being lifted for jacking equipment during lift-slab operations, which is a specific application. Other shoring standards, manufacturer requirements, or local rules may apply. The relevant shoring type and governing source must be confirmed before adding a design check.
