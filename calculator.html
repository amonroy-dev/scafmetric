Confirm these assumptions for this beam:

  • ASTM A992 steel, Fy = 50,000 psi and E = 29,000,000 psi.
  • Both flanges have adequate lateral and twist restraint at the supports.
  • Entered lateral-brace connections are rigid in the required restraint directions. The connected tubes, members and supports provide adequate brace strength and stiffness.
  • Loads push straight down over the center of the beam. Nothing pushes or pulls along the beam or applies a twisting force. The loading must not make the beam easier to tip sideways; loads on an unbraced top flange need a separate check.
  • Only the entered dead and live loads and the beam's self-weight are included. Wind, snow, rain, and earthquake loads are not added or checked separately. Any such load entered as dead or live load is treated only as a downward static load.

What is lateral bracing?

A bending steel beam can buckle sideways and twist. Lateral bracing restrains sideways movement of its compression flange; torsional bracing restrains twist. In this calculator's simply supported, downward-load model, the top flange is in compression. Effective bracing can improve buckling resistance; it does not reduce the calculated vertical sag.

What makes a brace effective?

  • Restrain the right flange. For lateral restraint, verify attachment near the compression flange. This model does not count a bottom-only restraint as a top-flange brace.
  • Check the whole arrangement. Trace forces from the beam through the clamps and tubes into the receiving beam, scaffold and supports. Paired beams can form an effective braced system when their lacing, diagonals or moment-resisting connections are designed together.
  • Check strength and stiffness. A brace must resist the force and limit movement. Include tube buckling or bending, clamp slip and rotation, connection offsets, and movement of the receiving structure.
  • Use the exact hardware instructions. Clamp number, placement, installation torque and permitted force direction depend on the component. Inspect for loose connections, damage and changes during erection or loading.

Sources and further reading

  • AISC Engineering Journal: Fundamentals of Beam Bracing — strength, stiffness and lateral/torsional restraint.
  • WorkSafe New Zealand, §13.5 — tube-and-coupler lacing and bracing of paired beams/trusses; contextual guidance, not a US spacing rule.
  • Uniscaff Technical Manual, printed page 5 — a manufactured diagonal brace using 1.9 × 0.095 tube; its ratings do not transfer to other assemblies.
  • BrandSafway CRA2B instructions — an example of component-specific installation and directional ratings, not a rating for unidentified clamps.
  • Experimental tube-and-fitting scaffold research — connection movement and cyclic response.
  • OSHA 1926.451 — scaffold design and inspection responsibilities.
  • Eng-Tips: Lateral/Torsional Bracing — practitioner discussion; use the primary guidance for design.
